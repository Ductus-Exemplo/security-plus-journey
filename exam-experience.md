# My CompTIA Security+ SY0-701 Exam Experience

**Certification:** CompTIA Security+ SY0-701  
**Result:** Passed  
**Date:** November 2026

## Introduction

In November 2026, I passed the CompTIA Security+ SY0-701 examination.

I'm sharing my personal preparation experience, the approach that worked for me, and what I would do differently if I were preparing again.

My biggest lesson was simple: **Practice makes perfect, but understanding your mistakes is just as important as getting questions right.**

## 1. How I Prepared

My preparation followed a domain-by-domain approach.

I used one main YouTube video course, starting from Domain 1 and working through to Domain 5.

After completing each domain, I attempted practice questions focused specifically on that domain.

Whenever I answered a question incorrectly or struggled to understand the explanation, I went back to review the relevant section.

I also used Professor Messer's videos for targeted revision rather than repeating an entire course.

Another resource I found useful was the practice question published on Professor Messer's website. When I got a question wrong, I used the reference to the relevant exam objective to revisit that topic.

Once I finished all five domains, I moved on to mixed practice questions.

You can find my [study method](study-method.md) and [learning resources](resources.md) in this repository.

## 2. Why Practice Questions Made a Difference

I answered a large number of multiple-choice questions from different YouTube resources and other practice materials.

By the time I sat the actual examination, the multiple-choice portion felt relatively straightforward.

I believe repeated practice played a major role in this.

As the saying goes, **practice makes perfect**.

But there's something important I learned along the way:

**Getting a question correct does not always mean you understand the concept.**

Sometimes I selected an answer because I was guessing between two options.

Even if my guess was correct, I treated that question as something I needed to review.

My approach was:

1. Answer the practice question.
2. Check whether my answer was correct.
3. Ask myself whether I genuinely understood why it was correct.
4. If I guessed or felt uncertain, review the relevant topic.
5. Understand why the other options were less suitable.

I recommend keeping track of both incorrect answers and correct answers that were based on guesses.

Both can reveal areas where you need more revision.

## 3. Practicing Under Timed Conditions

As part of my preparation, I used ChatGPT to create three full-length practice exams, each containing 90 questions.

For each mock exam, I gave myself **1 hour and 30 minutes**.

This helped me practice answering questions under time pressure and managing my pace.

I also reviewed my answers afterward to identify areas that needed additional attention.

For me, timed practice was useful because it made the process of working through a large set of mixed questions more familiar.

## 4. My Exam Experience

Having answered so many multiple-choice practice questions, I felt comfortable with that style of question during the examination.

The more challenging aspect for me was applying technical knowledge in practical situations.

This reinforced something important: knowing security terminology and recognizing correct answers is not the same as being able to investigate a system, understand its configuration, or interpret what is happening in a network.

I had spent some time reviewing Linux commands during my preparation, but I came away feeling that more practical familiarity with Linux would have helped.

Overall, the experience reminded me that **theoretical knowledge and practical understanding should complement each other**.

## 5. What I Would Do Differently

If I were preparing for Security+ again, I would keep my domain-by-domain learning strategy and continue doing plenty of practice questions.

However, I would devote additional time to the following areas.

### Security Log Analysis

I would spend more time learning how to interpret logs from different security systems, including:

- Firewall logs
- Linux and Windows system logs
- Authentication logs
- Network and security device logs

For example, I would practice examining log entries to identify suspicious activity, determine where an attack may have originated, and understand how events across different devices relate to one another.

The objective would be to develop a better understanding of what a security incident looks like in actual system records.

### Linux Commands and Security Administration

I would also dedicate more time to Linux administration and security-related commands.

Some areas I would focus on include:

- Identifying running processes and services
- Viewing active network connections and listening ports
- Examining authentication information
- Understanding user accounts and permissions
- Configuring secure authentication
- Identifying suspicious files or services
- Understanding how unwanted processes, files, or services can be safely removed during incident response

These skills are useful when investigating compromised systems and performing activities associated with incident response.

I would also connect this practical learning to the incident response lifecycle: **Preparation, Detection, Analysis, Containment, Eradication, Recovery, and Lessons Learned (PDACERL)**.

Understanding how to investigate an incident, contain it, remove the threat, and restore normal operations is important beyond simply knowing the definitions of the phases.

### Network Architecture and Device Placement

Another area I would spend more time exploring is how network security devices fit into an actual organizational environment.

It's one thing to know what a firewall, switch, web server, load balancer, or WAF does.

It's another thing to understand where those components should be placed and how they work together.

For example:

- Where should a firewall be positioned?
- Which servers should be accessible from the internet?
- Which systems should remain on protected internal networks?
- Where would a web application firewall be appropriate?
- How should different network segments communicate securely?

I would recommend spending some time studying network diagrams and understanding why security components are positioned where they are.

## 6. My Advice to Future Security+ Candidates

**Keep practicing questions.**

There is no harm in repeatedly testing your knowledge. Practice questions help reinforce what you've learned and reveal what you still need to understand.

However, don't focus only on your score.

Pay attention to questions where you guessed an answer, even when your guess turned out to be correct.

Also, remember that passing a cybersecurity certification involves more than memorizing terminology.

Try to understand how technologies are used, how systems communicate, and how security professionals investigate and respond to problems.

My personal recommendations are:

1. Use a structured main course.
2. Complete practice questions after each domain.
3. Review every incorrect or uncertain answer.
4. Use additional explanations when a concept isn't clear.
5. Practice full-length mixed exams under timed conditions.
6. Make some time for practical system administration, log analysis, and network architecture.

## 7. Final Thoughts

Passing CompTIA Security+ with a score of 797 was a rewarding milestone.

The preparation taught me the value of consistent practice, reviewing mistakes, and recognizing areas where I needed a better understanding.

If I could offer one piece of advice, it would be this:

**Don't just practice until you get the answer right. Practice until you understand why it's right.**

I hope sharing my experience helps someone else develop a study approach that works for them.
